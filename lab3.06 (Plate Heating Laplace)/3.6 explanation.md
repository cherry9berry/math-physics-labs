# Lab 3.6: Stationary Heat Conduction in a Rectangular Plate

## Work Description
This laboratory work investigates the steady-state temperature distribution in a rectangular plate. Mathematically, this corresponds to solving the **Laplace equation** $\Delta u = 0$ with specified boundary conditions (Dirichlet problem).

The goal is to determine the equilibrium temperature profile when the edges of the plate are held at fixed temperatures (represented as sine functions in this case).

## Mathematical Model
The problem resides on a rectangular domain $[0, L_x] \times [0, L_y]$:
$$\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} = 0$$
*   **Boundary Conditions:**
    *   $u(x, 0) = 0, \quad u(x, L_y) = A \sin(k_x x)$
    *   $u(0, y) = 0, \quad u(L_x, y) = B \sin(k_y y)$

### Solution Methods
1.  **Analytical Method (Separation of Variables):**
    The solution is sought as a sum of Fourier series. For these specific boundary conditions, the solution involves hyperbolic functions ($\sinh$) to account for the growth/decay from the boundaries:
    $$u(x, y) = \sum C_n \sinh(\alpha y) \sin(\beta x) + \sum D_n \sinh(\gamma x) \sin(\delta y)$$
2.  **Numerical Method (Relaxation/Steady-state flow):**
    The stationary solution can be found by treating it as the limit of a time-dependent heat equation. As $t \to \infty$, the distribution stabilizes. Practically, we use an explicit finite difference scheme on a rectangular grid and iterate until the change between steps (residual) drops below a threshold $\epsilon$.

## Implementation & Results
The notebook `3.6 Plate Heating Laplace.ipynb` demonstrates the convergence process. The numerical results are compared with the exact Fourier series solution. The 3D surface plot shows a smooth transition from the heated edges to the cool interior, illustrating the "averaging" property of the Laplace equation (where each point's value is the average of its neighbors).
