# Lab 3.4: The Stefan Problem (Moving Boundary)

## Work Description
This laboratory work investigates the **Stefan Problem**, a classic example of a phase change process (e.g., ice melting into water). The distinguishing feature of this problem is the presence of a **moving interface** $s(t)$ whose position is not known in advance and must be determined as part of the solution.

The goal is to model the temperature distribution in both phases and track the propagation of the melting front over time.

## Mathematical Model
We consider two regions: Ice ($x < s(t)$) and Water ($x > s(t)$). The heat equation holds in each:
$$\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = a_i^2 \frac{\partial^2 T}{\partial x^2}$$

### The Stefan Condition
At the interface $x = s(t)$, the temperature is constant ($T = T_{melt}$), and the energy balance (Stefan condition) determines the velocity of the front:
$$\rho \lambda \frac{ds}{dt} = k_1 \frac{\partial T_1}{\partial x} - k_2 \frac{\partial T_2}{\partial x}$$
where $\lambda$ is the latent heat of fusion.

### Solution Methods
1.  **Analytical Method:**
    The front position follows a square-root law: $s(t) = \alpha\sqrt{t}$. The parameter $\alpha$ is found by solving a transcendental equation derived from the flux balance.
2.  **Numerical Method (Enthalpy Formulation):**
    Instead of tracking the interface explicitly, we use the ** enthalpy method**. We solve for the total energy content (enthalpy $H$) on a fixed grid. The temperature $T(H)$ is then calculated based on whether the cell is ice, water, or in a "mushy" state (partially melted). This approach naturally handles the phase boundary movement without complex grid transformations.

## Implementation & Results
The notebook `3.4 Stefan Problem.ipynb` simulates the melting of a 10 cm layer of ice. The numerical model (using RK4 time-stepping) accurately predicts the front position and matches the analytical profile. The simulation shows that the melting rate decreases over time as the thermal gradient at the interface softens.
